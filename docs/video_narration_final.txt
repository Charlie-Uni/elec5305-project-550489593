01:52–02:15 | SLIDE 6 | Interference power alone does not predict failure
Visual: corresponding slide in the concise presentation.

Narration:
At plus five decibels, the SVM has about two percent macro-F1 under white noise. CLAP reaches about seventy-one percent under DEMAND but about forty-nine percent under same-category competition. Equal nominal power does not create equal difficulty. The main report shows all three levels with fold variation, and the supplement preserves the complete target-power counterpart.

02:15–02:39 | SLIDE 7 | Does robustness training transfer to competing events?
Visual: corresponding slide in the concise presentation.

Narration:
Across three CNN seeds, white-noise training raises white-noise macro-F1 from 7.48 to 43.95 percent, but same-category performance changes little. Semantic training reaches 36.05 percent while clean performance falls to 61.70. Direct semantic-over-white augmentation accuracy gains remain inconclusive after the declared correction. These results extend the literature’s augmentation question with measured transfer limits.

Evidence (not spoken): Extension protocol and independently audited intervention tables.

02:39–03:06 | SLIDE 8 | A captured decision can retain the target at rank two
Visual: corresponding slide in the concise presentation.

Narration:
Argmax forces one label. At own power and plus five decibels, 236 of 632 same-category captures retain the target second, or 37.34 percent. Different-category retention is 203 of 612, or 33.17 percent. The chart instead uses all trials as its denominator. Top-two ranking partially recovers the target; it is not a calibrated event detector. Fixed prompt ensembling also changes readout without retraining CLAP.
